Shipment Tracking

One reliable pipeline for shipment events from FedEx, DHL, and Pitney Bowes.

Python · PostgreSQL · AWS · DockerConcurrency · integrations · observability
Overview

Shipment tracking brought together multiple carrier APIs, scheduled jobs, webhooks, and internal order data. Each carrier spoke a different dialect, while the product needed one dependable view of an order’s status.

Decisions I owned +

I integrated DHL alongside FedEx and Pitney Bowes, mapping their responses into a carrier-agnostic internal model. I also refactored the status-checking job for clearer logging and error handling.

Batch PostgreSQL inserts and conflict-safe deduplication replaced row-by-row writes, while concurrent workers made the pipeline better suited to independent carrier requests.

Technical shape +

The system combined REST and webhook services, scheduled polling, response mapping, retention jobs, structured logs, Docker, and AWS deployment automation.

Outcome +

Shipment query latency moved from roughly 4 seconds to under 1 second, with a more maintainable path for adding carriers and handling duplicate events.
